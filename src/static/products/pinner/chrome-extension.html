Chrome extension

Pin images from any website, at the real resolution

Right-click an image, choose Pin image, and Pinner saves the biggest version it can find into an album, together with the page it came from, a colour palette and tags. It works in Chrome 116 or newer and in other Chromium browsers such as Edge, Brave and Arc.

How pinning works

  1. Right-click an imageOr hold the mouse over it and press Alt+Shift+S.
  2. Choose Pin image, then an albumPick Quick pin, Pin with details…, or any album, nested ones included. New album… makes one on the spot.
  3. Find it in your libraryOpen the library with Alt+Shift+L. Every pin remembers its source page.

A right-click on a link to an image gives Pin linked image. A right-click on a page gives Pin background image under cursor and Pick images from this page…. If you prefer, switch on a small Pin button that appears on large images.

It gets the real image, not the thumbnail

Most sites show a small copy of a picture and hide the large one. Pinner goes looking for it.

Reads the page properly

Picks the largest srcset or <picture> source, understands lazy-loading attributes and follows links to the full-size file.

Knows the thumbnail tricks

Turns the small addresses used by many sites back into the full-size original. See the list below.

Has a plan B and a plan C

If a site blocks downloads, Pinner fetches the image from inside the page, and as a last resort takes a picture of it.

Tidy while it saves

No duplicates

Pinner fingerprints every image (SHA-256), so it can tell when you have already pinned the same picture.

A colour palette

Each pin gets a palette, so you can search your library by colour.

Formats converted

AVIF, HEIC, SVG, BMP and TIFF can be converted to JPEG or PNG as they are saved, and large images can be scaled down.

Tags and clean links

Can tag pins by the site they came from, and strip tracking parameters from their addresses.

A full library in your browser

Press Alt+Shift+L for a library page with everything you need to find a picture again.

  • Nested albums you can re-parent by dragging
  • Masonry, grid and list views, with virtual scrolling for large libraries
  • Filters by rating, shape, colour, site and tag, and favourites
  • Bulk move, tag, rate and export
  • A lightbox, and a trash that empties itself
  • Drag-and-drop or Ctrl+V to import
  • ZIP exports, and full backup and restore

Scout talent as you browse

Choose Scout talent… and the pin dialog opens in scouting mode: name, Instagram, agency, height, city, contact and notes. On Instagram pages the handle and display name are filled in from the page.

In Pinner Studio each scouted pin becomes a talent profile with the status Scouted, or the photo is added to the profile you already have if the name or Instagram handle matches.

Works alone, better with Pinner Studio

The extension keeps its own library in the browser, and it never needs an account. When Pinner Studio is running on the same computer, the two pair by themselves and pins flow into Studio. Pins made while Studio is not running wait in a queue until it is. How pairing works.

What it asks for, and why

Pinner asks for access to all sites because its job is to let you right-click an image anywhere, show the album menu on the page and add Mark location for shoot to Google Maps. It uses that access only when you ask. It does not read, record or send your browsing, forms, passwords or history. Page addresses, titles and the images you pin are stored on your own device.
